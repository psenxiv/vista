# The timing graph

The timing graph shows how the camera moves along a track over time. Use it to see how fast the camera goes at each moment, and where it stops.

Click the chart icon, **Timing**, on the top row of the **Vista** window to open the **Timing** window. Click it again to close it. The graph needs at least two points on the track. You can look at the graph in any mode, but you can only change it in Edit mode.

## Reading the graph

Time runs from left to right, in seconds, along the strip at the bottom. The number at the right end of the strip is the length of the whole track. Distance along the track runs from bottom to top, in yalms, with its scale on the right.

The line is the camera's journey. Where it is steep, the camera moves fast. Where it is flat, the camera is standing still.

- Each point has a faint line across the graph, with the point's number at the left.
- Each point is a key: a numbered circle on the line, where the camera reaches that point.
- A hold shows as a flat stretch that ends in a small dot. That dot is also a key.
- A vertical line marks the current moment in the track: where playback is, or where you last moved to.

Hold the mouse over the line to see a tooltip with the time, the distance travelled and the camera's speed at that moment.

To change the line, see Keys and easing.

## Moving the camera through time

In Edit mode, click or drag in the strip along the bottom of the graph to move the camera to that moment in the track. When you let go, you can fly on from where the camera is.

## Zooming and moving the view

To see keys that sit close together, zoom in. Hold the mouse over the graph and scroll up. The graph zooms in around the mouse, and the distance scale changes to fit what you can see. Scroll down to zoom out.

When you are zoomed in, drag an empty part of the graph to move the view sideways. Click the **Show the whole track** button, at the top right of the window, to see the whole track again.
