# Adding points

To add a point, fly the camera to where you want it and aim it at what you want to see. Then add the point. It records the camera as it is now: its position, its aim (which way it faces), its roll (how far it tilts to one side) and its field of view, or FoV (how wide it sees).

You add points in Edit mode.

## The add button

The add button is the plus on the track row. Click **Add point** to add a point at the end of the track.

The small arrow beside it is **More ways to add**. It opens a menu:

| Menu item | Key | Does |
|---|---|---|
| **Add to end** | `Backtick` | Adds a point after the last one. |
| **Add after selected** | `Alt + Backtick` | Adds a point straight after the selected one, and selects the new point. |
| **Overwrite selected** | `Ctrl + Backtick` | Replaces the selected point with the camera as it is now. The point keeps its place and its timing. |

The last two need a selected point, so they are greyed out until you select one.

If a track is playing when you add a point, the point records the frame on screen. You can't add a point while you drag the scrub bar.

A track's first point also places its anchor, and the scene's anchor if it has none yet. Scenes and anchors explains what they are.

## The points table

The table in the middle of the **Vista** window lists the track's points, one row each. The number on the left is the point's number, which also shows beside the point in the game world.

- Click a row to select that point. You can also click the point in the game world. Click empty space in the world to clear the selection.
- Double-click a row to move the camera to that point, as the track would show it.
- Drag a row onto another row to move the point to that place in the track.
- Hold the mouse over a row and click the trash button at its right end (**Delete point**) to delete it. Press `Delete` or `Backspace` to delete the selected point.

When a point is selected, the **Point** window opens so you can change it. The Point window page covers it.

The **Duration (s)**, **Speed** and **Hold (s)** columns and the pins set the track's timing, and Legs and holds explains them.
